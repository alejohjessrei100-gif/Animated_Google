# The winning formula: "Gold Down the Well"

"The Well That Was Never Yours" got 102% average retention and 80% stayed to watch. Every new script keeps these ingredients.

## Why it worked
1. **A treasure plus a mundane hiding place, in 11 words.** "If you find gold down a well, you have a vault." Viewers instantly know the prize and the place.
2. **The story goes down.** Every "So you..." digs, floods or tunnels, so the escalations stack visibly in one cutaway. The underground *is* the story, so the cutaway is always on screen for a reason.
3. **Gold on screen the whole time.** The prize never leaves the frame, so viewers keep watching to see what happens to it.
4. **Small-time hero against a king or guards.** It's an underdog with a stash against authority.
5. **Trio: animal, animal, cousin Steve.** Each gets a one-line gag, and Steve is the human who ruins it.
6. **A "Wait. Why?" beat about 34 s in.** It re-hooks viewers right before the twist.
7. **Twist: "not there by mistake".** The place was set up by someone more powerful, and the hero was a pawn, or the heir.
8. **The last line calls back the hook noun** ("vault", "door", "castle") and the last shot returns to the first shot, so the loop is seamless and rewatches stack.

## Rules for the next scripts
- **Copy the engine, never the premise.** No more gold, vaults or hiding things. The well worked because of its structure, not its noun.
- **One place that visibly transforms.** A hole, a house, a tree or a tower grows with every "So you...", in one continuous stage.
- **The twist is a camera reveal.** The last beat shows something that was in the world all along (the sunk floors, the digger on the other side). The cutaway opens only for it, or for digging.
- **Hero:** Gus is "you". Cousin Steve recurs in every trio.
- **Length:** 150–170 words, the "But… So you…" engine, with the twist landing at 33–36 s.
